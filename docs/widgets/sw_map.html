Description

The world at five degrees per cell: a built-in Natural Earth 110m land raster, drawn as quiet cells - COARSE AND HONEST (real coastline data is a stated future; the raster was authored from geography and its assault quizzes it: Denver is land, the mid-Atlantic is sea, the Sahara is land, Australia is land). The projection math is public and assaulted: x_of_lon / y_of_lat and their inverses round-trip exactly; the dateline is the left edge, Greenwich the middle, the pole the top.

Markers carry labels at lat/lon and answer hover within reach (marker_at). Elsewhere the hover chip reads out lat / lon / land-or-sea - the map answers where you are, always. And the timezone band-map grew up as promised: highlight_utc washes the 15-degree band of any UTC offset with its centre meridian marked - the SW_TIMEZONE_PICKER's foundation is now real.

Markers and a zone

map.highlight_utc (-7)
page.put (map.with_title (class="string">"the world"))

map.x_of_lon (-105.0)      -- projection, public
map.is_land (25.0, 10.0)   -- the Sahara says True

Known limits

  • Five-degree cells: coastlines are blocky by design. Real polygon data (and a second projection) are the stated future.
  • Equirectangular only - poles stretch, as they do.
  • One highlighted zone at a time.

Bugs & gotchas

  • The raster is a truth about CELLS: is_land answers for the whole 5-degree box, so islands smaller than a cell may not exist yet.
  • highlight_utc bands are nominal meridians - real-world zone borders wobble politically; this draws the geometry, not the law.

Extension plans & possibilities

  • Real coastline polygons; SW_TIMEZONE_PICKER riding this map with live per-zone clocks (the pinned timezone-tools idea); marker clustering.
