Description

SW_SCALE is a linear map from a data DOMAIN onto a pixel RANGE, its inverse (hover asks "what value is under the pointer?"), and the classic 1/2/5 tick ladder - steps are always 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, so gridlines land on numbers a human would choose. Every chart in the toolkit (bars, lines, sparklines, heatmaps, gauges) shares this one engine rather than reinventing axis arithmetic.

Degenerate domains answer honestly: when min = max, positions collapse to the range midpoint and the ladder offers the one value - no division by zero, no NaN smuggling.

Source: src/sw_scale.e · the charts' substrate since Wave 4. Pure math, no painter - which is why every claim is assaulted headless.

Domain to pixels and back

local
    s: SW_SCALE
do
    create s.make (0.0, 250.0, 40.0, 640.0)
    -- or retarget live: s.set_domain (...), s.set_range (...)
    -- position (125.0) = 340.0 (the middle maps to the middle)
    -- value_at (340.0) = 125.0 (the inverse agrees)
    -- ticks (6) yields 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250 (the 1/2/5 ladder)
    -- nice_domain (6) snaps min/max outward onto ladder values
end

Known limits

  • Linear only - log and time scales are planned as siblings, not modes.
  • ticks (n) aims NEAR n via the 1/2/5 ladder - exact counts are not guaranteed; landing on human numbers is the point.

Bugs & gotchas

  • Set the domain from your DATA and the range from your PIXELS - swapping them produces a map that "works" but draws everything at one edge.
  • A reversed range (right-to-left, bottom-to-top axes) is legal and useful - y-axes typically map min to the BOTTOM pixel, which is the larger number.

Future

  • SW_LOG_SCALE and SW_TIME_SCALE siblings sharing the same interface.
